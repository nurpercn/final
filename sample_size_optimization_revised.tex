\subsection{Sample-Size Optimization}
\label{subsec:sample_optimization}
\label{subsec:sample_size_optimization}

In this stage, we refine the sample-size vector for a given chamber configuration assignment. More specifically, we consider increasing or decreasing the number of samples for each product by up to two (without violating \(s_{\min}\) and \(s_{\max}\)). For each of these possible local moves, we use the scheduling procedure to determine the schedule and observe the effect of such a local move on the total tardiness. For each product, we update the sample size if the solution improves. Then, we check the late and early products and consider increasing the sample size of a late product and decreasing the sample size of an early product by one. If such a move improves the solution, we update it. These two local search moves (changing the sample size of a single product and changing the sample sizes of a pair of late and early products) are implemented sequentially as long as the solution improves. The steps of the local search procedure for improving the sample-size decisions are provided in Algorithm~\ref{alg:sample_ls}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{SampleSizeLocalSearch}()}
\label{alg:sample_ls}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} chamber configuration assignment \(\mathbf{x}\), sample-size vector \(\mathbf{s}\)
\State \textbf{Output:} an improved sample-size vector and a feasible schedule \((\mathbf{s},\Pi,T^{tot})\)
\State \((\Pi,T^{tot})\leftarrow\Call{Schedule}{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}}\) \label{step:ls-eval}
\Repeat
    \For{\(p\in\mathcal{P}\)} \label{step:ls-loop1}
        \For{\(s'\in\{s_p-2,s_p-1,s_p+1,s_p+2\}\cap[s_{\min},s_{\max}]\)}
            \State \((\Pi_{s'},T^{tot}_{s'})\leftarrow\Call{Schedule}{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}'}\) \Comment{\(\mathbf{s}'=\mathbf{s}\) with the \(p\)th component equal to \(s'\)} \label{step:ls-nbhd}
        \EndFor
        \State \(s^{\star}\leftarrow\arg\min\{T^{tot}_{s'}\}\), breaking ties by smaller \(s'\) \label{step:ls-nbhd2}
        \If{\((T^{tot},\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{s})\succ_{\mathrm{lex}}(T^{tot}_{s^{\star}},\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{s}^{\star})\)} \Comment{\(\mathbf{s}^{\star}=\mathbf{s}\) with the \(p\)th component equal to \(s^{\star}\)}
            \State \(s_p\leftarrow s^{\star}\) \label{step:ls-accept}
            \State \((\Pi,T^{tot})\leftarrow(\Pi_{s^{\star}},T^{tot}_{s^{\star}})\) \label{step:ls-loop1.5}
        \EndIf
    \EndFor \label{step:ls-loop2}
    \State \(\mathbf{s}_{reb}\leftarrow\Call{LateEarlyRebalance}{\mathbf{s},t_{\mathrm{reb}}}\) \label{step:ls-reb}
    \State \((\Pi_{reb},T^{tot}_{reb})\leftarrow\Call{Schedule}{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}_{reb}}\) \label{step:ls-reb2}
    \If{\((T^{tot},\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{s})\succ_{\mathrm{lex}}(T^{tot}_{reb},\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{s}_{reb})\)}
        \State \((\mathbf{s},\Pi,T^{tot})\leftarrow(\mathbf{s}_{reb},\Pi_{reb},T^{tot}_{reb})\) \label{step:ls-reb3}
    \EndIf
\Until{no improvement} \label{step:ls-stop}
\State \Return \((\mathbf{s},\Pi,T^{tot})\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

In Algorithm~\ref{alg:sample_ls}, we start with a schedule determined for the given chamber configuration assignment and sample-size decisions (Step~\ref{step:ls-eval}). Then, we check whether increasing or decreasing the number of samples of a product by up to two units improves the current solution (Steps~\ref{step:ls-loop1}--\ref{step:ls-loop2}). For each such feasible move we call the scheduling procedure (Step~\ref{step:ls-nbhd}). We then determine the best move for each product (Step~\ref{step:ls-nbhd2}) and update the current solution if that move improves it under the lexicographic test (Steps~\ref{step:ls-accept}--\ref{step:ls-loop1.5}). Next, \(t_{\mathrm{reb}}\) late and early product pairs are chosen and their sample sizes are increased and decreased by one unit (Step~\ref{step:ls-reb}). If the new schedule after this step is better than the current one, the current solution is updated (Steps~\ref{step:ls-reb2}--\ref{step:ls-reb3}). The two moves are repeated until a pass yields no improvement.
