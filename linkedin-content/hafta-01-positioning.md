## Post (kopyala-yapıştır)

Most people think regulatory affairs is about ticking boxes.

In practice, knowing a regulation or a standard is never enough.

Regulatory affairs is as much about judgement as it is about technical knowledge. Reading is not the same as interpreting. And meaningful interpretation requires a deep understanding of the product.

A requirement rarely exists in isolation. It needs to be considered alongside:
• product parameters and design choices
• related standards and interacting regulatory requirements
• test evidence, certification routes and national deviations
• commercial realities — timelines, cost, supply continuity and market access

This is where regulatory work becomes truly cross-functional. It requires close coordination with R&D, Quality, laboratories, Supply Chain and commercial teams to turn ambiguity into clear, actionable guidance: what must be met, how it can be achieved, where the risks are, and what trade-offs the business is actually making.

Over the past several years at Vestel, I’ve led global regulatory affairs and market access activities for refrigeration products across 100+ countries — managing approximately 500 testing, reporting and certification projects annually, supporting thousands of active product codes, and continuously navigating CE, UKCA, CB, UL, BIS, G-Mark and global energy-efficiency requirements.

Alongside this, I’m completing a PhD in Industrial Engineering at Özyeğin University, focused on optimisation-based test chamber scheduling. For me, there is a natural connection between the two: effective compliance requires not only technical expertise, but also better processes, better prioritisation and better decision-making systems.

The greatest value of Regulatory Affairs comes from helping an organisation understand the whole picture and make informed decisions — not simply from quoting a clause.
