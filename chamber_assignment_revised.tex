\subsection{Chamber assignment under environment constraints}
\label{subsec:chamber_assignment}

In this section, we explain how the chamber configurations are
determined based on the test workload.
We use \(\mathbf{s}=(s_p)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\) to represent the
sample-size vector.
For a fixed sample-size vector \(\mathbf{s}\), we determine the
environmental configuration of each chamber by a two-step
procedure: a mixed-integer linear program that matches station
counts to workload-based targets, followed by a local search that
improves the resulting assignment against total tardiness.
Since sample counts affect the workload of environmental
configurations, chamber assignment must be updated when
\(\mathbf{s}\) changes.
We use \(m_{pt}(s_p)\) to represent the number of times test \(t\)
of product \(p\) has to be executed.
We first determine the total test workload for each environmental
configuration based on the voltage-adjustment requirement.
More specifically, for each environmental configuration
\(e\in\mathcal{E}\) we calculate the overall total test duration
and the total test duration with nonstandard voltage as follows:
\begin{align*}
W_e(\mathbf{s}) &=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
\sum_{\substack{t\in\mathcal{T}_p\\e(t)=e}}
m_{pt}(s_p)\tau_t,
\\
W_e^V(\mathbf{s}) &=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}^v}
\sum_{\substack{t\in\mathcal{T}_p\\e(t)=e}}
m_{pt}(s_p)\tau_t.
\end{align*}
The workload shares of each environmental configuration,
considering all tests and only those requiring nonstandard
voltage, respectively, are calculated as follows:
\[
\phi_e=\frac{W_e(\mathbf{s})}{\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}}W_e(\mathbf{s})},\qquad
\phi_e^V=\frac{W_e^V(\mathbf{s})}{\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}}W_e^V(\mathbf{s})}.
\]
If no test requires nonstandard voltage, we set
\(\phi_e^V=0\).
The target capacities (in terms of number of stations required)
are calculated as follows:
\[
S_e=\phi_e \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}K_c,\qquad
S_e^V=\phi_e^V \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}^v}K_c.
\]

We define \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{s}}\) and
\(\mathcal{E}^V_{\mathbf{s}}\) as the sets of environmental
conditions for which nonzero test durations are needed,
considering all tests and the ones requiring nonstandard voltage
only. That is,
\[
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{s}}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:\ W_e(\mathbf{s})>0\},
\]
\[
\mathcal{E}^V_{\mathbf{s}}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:W_e^V(\mathbf{s})>0\}.
\]
The parameters and decision variables of the chamber
configuration model are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:notation_ccd}.

Using the workload calculations explained above, we propose a
mixed-integer linear program to determine the configurations of
the chambers.
Our goal in this model is to minimize the summation of the
differences between the number of stations assigned to a
configuration and the target capacity for that configuration.
The proposed chamber configuration determination model is as
follows:
\begin{align}
\textrm{MIP-CCD($\mathbf{s}$)}: \min\quad
&
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}z_e+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}z_e^V
\label{eq:assign_obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}x_{ce}=1,
&& \forall c\in\mathcal{C},
\label{eq:assign_one}\\
&
\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}x_{ce}\ge 1,
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{s}},
\label{eq:assign_cover}\\
&
\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}^v}x_{ce}\ge 1,
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E}^V_{\mathbf{s}},
\label{eq:assign_volt}\\
&
x_{ce}\le h_c,
&& \forall c\in\mathcal{C},\ e\in\mathcal{E}^H,
\label{eq:assign_hum}\\
&
z_e\ge \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}K_cx_{ce}-S_e,
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\label{eq:assign_z1}\\
&
z_e\ge S_e-\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}K_cx_{ce},
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\label{eq:assign_z2}\\
&
z_e^V\ge \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}^v}K_cx_{ce}-S_e^V,
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\label{eq:assign_zv1}\\
&
z_e^V\ge S_e^V-\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}^v}K_cx_{ce},
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\label{eq:assign_zv2}\\
&
x_{ce}\in\{0,1\},
&& \forall c\in\mathcal{C},\ e\in\mathcal{E},
\label{eq:assign_bin}\\
&
z_e,z_e^V\ge 0,
&& \forall e\in\mathcal{E}.
\label{eq:assign_dom}
\end{align}
In this model, objective~\eqref{eq:assign_obj} minimizes the total
deviation between assigned station counts and the two target
capacities.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:assign_one} assigns each chamber to exactly
one environmental configuration.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:assign_cover} gives every configuration with
a positive workload at least one chamber.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:assign_volt} gives every configuration with
a positive nonstandard-voltage workload at least one
voltage-capable chamber.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:assign_hum} forbids humidity-controlled
configurations on chambers that cannot hold humidity.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:assign_z1}--\eqref{eq:assign_z2} force
\(z_e\) to be at least the absolute difference between the number
of stations assigned to \(e\) and \(S_e\).
Constraints~\eqref{eq:assign_zv1}--\eqref{eq:assign_zv2} do the
same for voltage-capable stations and \(S_e^V\).
The remaining constraints specify the domains of the decision
variables.

After the chamber configurations are determined, as a second step
we implement a local search phase to fine-tune the chamber
configuration decisions.
Let \((\Pi,T^{tot})=\mathrm{Schedule}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\) be
the earliest-due-date schedule of the current assignment, and let
\(B_c\) be the total processing time placed in chamber \(c\) by
\(\Pi\).
The \emph{normalized workload} of chamber \(c\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chamber_load}
\lambda_c=\frac{B_c}{K_c}.
\end{equation}
Dividing by \(K_c\) makes chambers of different sizes comparable.
A chamber is \emph{highly loaded} if its \(\lambda_c\) is large
and \emph{lightly loaded} if its \(\lambda_c\) is small.
Neighboring assignments are generated from this ranking.
A swap exchanges the environments of a highly loaded chamber
\(c\) and a lightly loaded chamber \(c'\) with
\(\lambda_c>\lambda_{c'}\), provided both chambers can hold the
other's setting.
A move reassigns a lightly loaded chamber to the environment of a
highly loaded chamber.
Both operators are retained only if they still satisfy
\eqref{eq:assign_one}--\eqref{eq:assign_hum}.
The acceptance criterion is total tardiness: the first feasible
neighbor whose EDD schedule has a strictly smaller \(T^{tot}\) is
accepted, and the search continues from that assignment with
updated loads \(\lambda_c\).
Swap is tried first.
Move is used only when a full scan of the swap pairs accepts
nothing.
The search stops when a pass of swap and then move finds no
improving feasible neighbor.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:chamber_assignment} is the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ChamberAssignment}
\label{alg:chamber_assignment}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} sample vector \(\mathbf{s}\)
\State \textbf{Output:} chamber assignment \(\mathbf{x}\)
\State Compute \(W_e(\mathbf{s})\), \(W_e^V(\mathbf{s})\), \(\phi_e\), \(\phi_e^V\), \(S_e\), and \(S_e^V\) \label{step:ca-load}
\State \(\mathbf{x}\leftarrow\) the optimal solution of MIP-CCD(\(\mathbf{s}\)) \label{step:ca-exact}
\Repeat \label{step:ca-loop}
    \State Compute \(\lambda_c\) from \(\mathrm{Schedule}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})\) \label{step:ca-loadrank}
    \State Try swaps of highly loaded and lightly loaded chambers that stay feasible \label{step:ca-nbhd}
    \If{no swap reduces \(T^{tot}\)}
        \State Try moves of a lightly loaded chamber onto a highly loaded environment
    \EndIf
    \If{a neighbor reduces \(T^{tot}\)} \label{step:ca-accept}
        \State Replace \(\mathbf{x}\) by that neighbor
    \EndIf
\Until{no improving feasible neighbor remains} \label{step:ca-stop}
\State \Return \(\mathbf{x}\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Step~\ref{step:ca-load} forms the two workloads, the shares, and
the station targets.
Step~\ref{step:ca-exact} solves MIP-CCD(\(\mathbf{s}\)).
Step~\ref{step:ca-loadrank} ranks chambers by
\eqref{eq:chamber_load}.
Steps~\ref{step:ca-nbhd}--\ref{step:ca-accept} accept the first
feasible swap, or move, that reduces tardiness, and stop when a
pass finds none (Step~\ref{step:ca-stop}).
