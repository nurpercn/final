\subsection{Instance Generation}
\label{subsec:exp_design}
\label{subsec:experimental_design}

We generate test instances motivated by current industrial practice.
The computational study uses a structured scenario set designed to
capture the main sources of variability in industrial test-chamber
operations: product test requirements, due-date pressure, and voltage
compatibility.
Each instance represents a multi-project testing scenario in which
products differ in required tests, due dates, and voltage requirements.

At a given planning time, the system is expected to have 50 products
under test.
For this reason, every instance contains 50 products.
We consider three due-date scenarios and three nonstandard-voltage
scenarios.
The due dates lie within a two-month planning horizon and are generated
at three levels of tightness.
Scenario~1 is relatively concentrated, with due dates ranging from 42
to 57 days and a median around 50 days.
Scenario~2 introduces stronger dispersion and earlier deadlines, with
due dates ranging from 30 to 69 days and a median around 50 days.
Scenario~3 is more relaxed but still heterogeneous, with due dates
ranging from 34 to 69 days and a median around 53 days.

The voltage scenarios control the proportion of products that require
voltage-compatible execution, using the mix in current operations as
the reference.
Scenario~1 represents a low voltage-adjustment requirement, where
approximately 15\% of the products require nonstandard voltage.
Scenario~2 represents a medium requirement, with 30\% of the products
requiring nonstandard voltage.
Scenario~3 represents a higher requirement, with 45\% of the products
requiring nonstandard voltage.
Since voltage feasibility is one-sided, increasing this share reduces
the effective resource pool and creates stronger competition for
chambers that can adjust voltage.

These choices give nine combinations of due-date and voltage scenarios.
For each combination we generate 10 instances with different product
test requirements, so the study uses 90 instances in total.
For each product, refrigerant charge determination, pull-down, the
performance test at \(25^\circ\mathrm{C}\), and the three consumer-usage
tests are required.
The energy-efficiency option and the remaining performance, freezing,
and temperature-rise tests are selected as explained under
Table~\ref{tab:test_attributes}.
The reported results cover all 90 instances and are also discussed
with respect to these due-date and voltage scenarios.
