## 5. Safety Compliance — teknik sorular

### “What does a Safety Compliance Engineer do, in your words?”

> You make sure the product that leaves the factory is the same product that was assessed. That means the right directives, the right standards, the right tests, a living technical file, and a clear stop when something is not ready. You are not a policeman at the end of the line. You sit with development from the beginning, and you stay until certification, production and any later change are under control.

---

### “Explain CE marking for a new heat pump platform.”

Bunu akış olarak anlat; ezber mevzuat dersi değil.

> I start with intended use, installation, voltage, connectivity, and whether we are in household or more machinery-like territory. From that I list the applicable directives — typically LVD, EMC, RoHS, and depending on the product RED, Machinery, and for the pressure side PED. Then the harmonised standards: 60335-1 plus 2-40 for heat pumps, EMC standards, and whatever else the risk assessment points to.
>
> Then a gap analysis against the current design: construction, creepage and clearance, protective earthing, abnormal operation, refrigerant charge and leak scenarios, marking, manuals. I turn that into a test plan — what we can do in-house, what goes to an external lab — and I get quotations and a protocol before we send samples.
>
> After testing, we close non-conformities with R&D, retest what matters, compile the technical file, and only then the Declaration of Conformity and CE marking. Production is not released on a draft report. And any later component change goes through the same impact assessment. That’s the part people skip, and that’s where you get market-surveillance problems.

**Türkçe iskelet:** kullanım → direktifler → standartlar → gap → test planı → lab → uygunsuzluk kapatma → teknik dosya → DoC/CE → üretim izni → değişiklik kontrolü.

---

### “LVD vs RED vs Machinery Directive — how do they sit on a heat pump?”

Dürüst sınır.

> LVD is electrical safety for equipment in the voltage range. For household heat pumps, IEC/EN 60335 is the normal route to presumption of conformity. That’s my strongest area.
>
> RED applies if the product places radio equipment on the market — Wi-Fi, cellular, proprietary RF for monitoring. Then you have radio essential requirements on top of electrical safety and EMC, and the radio module’s own certification is not automatically the finished-product assessment. I have worked with EMC and with radio modules as critical components in safety reports. I have not been the RED owner. I would not pretend otherwise.
>
> Machinery Directive is about machinery risks: moving parts, access, emergency stop, ISO 12100 type thinking. Some heat pumps, especially larger or more commercial systems, sit closer to MD; many household appliances sit under LVD. The first job is the correct scope decision, not forcing every product into one box. I would align that with NIBE’s existing classification, not invent a new one on day one.

---

### “What do you know about IEC 60335-2-40?”

Bilmediğini gizleme; aileyi göster.

> 60335-2-40 is the Part 2 for electrical heat pumps, air-conditioners and dehumidifiers. It sits on 60335-1 and adds the product-specific hazards: refrigerant, tightness, leak simulation, charge limits by flammability class, ventilation, marking, and installation instructions. The 2022/2024 updates are very much about A2L and A3 refrigerants.
>
> My daily standard has been 60335-2-24 for refrigerators, which already includes flammable refrigerant construction and charge logic, typically R600a. I have not run 2-40 as the lead standard. The method is the same: Part 1 plus Part 2, national deviations, test plan, critical components, marking. I would spend the first weeks mapping 2-40 clause by clause against NIBE’s current platforms with the test team.

İstersen bir cümle daha: F-gas geçişi yüzünden bu standardın NIBE için stratejik olduğunu anlıyorsun.

---

### “How do you work with external laboratories and certification bodies?”

> I treat them as partners, not as a mailbox. Before we send anything I freeze the scope: standard version, sample configuration, critical components, which tests are delta versus full. I ask for a quotation against that protocol, not a vague “please test the product.”
>
> I do not send a sample I already know will fail. That wastes money and time — I had that argument in the China transition. During the project I answer technical questions quickly and I escalate ambiguities in a call, not a 20-mail chain. After the report I check that the photos, ratings, components and standard editions actually match the production unit. Then I keep the relationship warm so that when we have an urgent case, they will prioritise us. I did that with Gulf bodies and with GSO when we had a tender at risk.

Kuruluşlar: UL Solutions, Intertek, SGS, TÜV Rheinland, TÜV SÜD, CVC. İsveç’te muhtemelen Intertek, RISE, TÜV, Nemko, UL — “benim listem sizin listeniz olmayabilir, yöntemin aynı.”

---

### “A safety test fails two weeks before launch. What do you do?”

Bu soru karakter sorusu. Doğru cevap: durdurursun.

> I stop the launch path for that configuration. Hope is not a control measure. I contain: which units, which factories, which markets, is anything already produced.
>
> Then root cause with R&D — is it construction, a component, a test setup, or a wrong sample. We agree the fix, we retest the relevant parts, we update the file. If the date cannot be met, I say so early, with the residual risk in plain language, so sales is not promising something we cannot ship.
>
> I’ve had sales and customers call this blocking. Over time they saw I say yes whenever the risk is manageable, and I only stop when the downside is non-compliance, a failed audit, or a product in the field we cannot defend.

---

### “How do you maintain the technical file?”

> The file is not a PDF folder from the first CE date. It has the risk assessment, the list of applicable directives and standards, drawings, critical component list with certificates, test reports with the correct edition, manuals, marking artwork, DoC, and the change history.
>
> My obsession is consistency: rating label, marking, report, certificate, and the unit on the line must tell the same story. At Vestel I went through about 5,000 active codes because rated current on the labels had drifted inside the same model family. That’s the kind of error that fails an inspection a year later.

---

### “How do you monitor new legislation and standards?”

> Habit, not heroics. At Vestel I started the day with the Official Journal. I sat in APPLiA working groups on safety and energy. I tracked draft IEC/EN texts with our internal experts for TC61 and related committees. When the mandatory date for a new EU safety standard was published, I saw it in the OJ before the APPLiA circulate — and I shared it.
>
> Then I translate: what changes in construction, test, marking, which platforms, which stocks, which certificate updates. A monitoring mail that nobody can act on is useless.

---

### “How would you train colleagues here?”

> Short, practical, audience-specific. Sales needs: what blocks a shipment, lead times, what they must not promise. Designers need: which clauses change the copper and the plastic, not a 200-page standard dump. I did this for new sales people by market, and I mentored a colleague who came into certification with no background.
>
> I would not walk into NIBE and retrain everyone in month one. First I learn your existing process, then I fill gaps.

---

### “EMC, RoHS, REACH — is that in scope?”

Safety ilanı LVD/RED/MD diyor; senin geçmişin daha geniş. Kısa tut.

> In Vestel’s product safety team they sat together: LVD, EMC, RoHS, REACH, POP, PFAS. I can support those interfaces. I would not steal the environmental specialist’s job if NIBE splits it. I just won’t let a safety file ignore a chemical or EMC change that invalidates the assessment.

### “What about Ecodesign and energy labelling for heat pumps?”

> I ran energy compliance for refrigerators — EU Ecodesign and labelling, IEC 62552, DOE, NRCan, ENERGY STAR, China CEL, India BEE. Heat-pump Ecodesign is a different regulation set, but the operating model is the same: test conditions, declared values, label, technical documentation, and no production until the numbers match. ## 6. PED görüşmesi olursa

### Açılış cümlesi (zorunlu dürüstlük)

> I want to be straight: I have not been a PED owner. I have not classified pressure equipment under 2014/68/EU or chosen conformity modules with a notified body for PED. What I do bring is CE practice, electrical safety, test and certification management, and years on products that are sealed refrigeration systems with compressors and heat exchangers. I would treat PED as a discipline to learn properly, not as something I already stamp.

### “What do you know about PED anyway?”

Yüzeysel ezberden iyisi: çerçeve + öğrenme planı.

> PED covers the design and manufacture of pressure equipment above certain thresholds. Fluids are grouped — Group 1 hazardous, Group 2 the rest — and equipment is categorised from SEP up to Category IV based on pressure, volume or DN, and fluid group. The category drives the module: self-assessment versus notified-body involvement, up to full quality assurance.
>
> For heat pumps the practical questions are: is it a piece of equipment or an assembly, which parts are in scope, which are excluded, and how EN 378 sits next to PED for refrigerating systems. Flammable refrigerants push you toward Group 1 thinking. I know that map. I have not yet walked a NIBE product through it. My first task would be to sit with your current PED files and the notified body you already use, and learn the house rules before I change anything.

Söyleme: “Category III module H1’i ben yönetirim.”

### PED + elektriksel güvenlik + makine — ilanın üçlüsü

> The job posting puts three things together: pressure equipment law, electrical safety, and machinery. That’s a system product, not a single directive. My strongest leg is electrical safety and the CE operating system. Machinery and PED are the legs I would build with the Markaryd specialists. I’m used to being the person who keeps the whole file coherent so we don’t CE-mark on LVD while the pressure assembly is still open.

### “EN 378 vs 60335-2-40 vs PED”

> They answer different questions. 60335-2-40 is product safety of the appliance. EN 378 is refrigerating systems — safety and environment — design, charge, location, installation. PED is the legal pressure-equipment framework. You need a consistent story across all three, especially with A2L/A3 refrigerants. I would not pick a fight between standards; I would map overlaps and make sure the technical file doesn’t contradict itself.

## 7. Davranışsal sorular — STAR, konuşma dilinde

Her hikâyede: Situation kısa, Task bir cümle, Action senin yaptığın, Result sayı veya somut sonuç. “We” içinde “I”yi kaybetme; İsveç’te de katkı net olsun, ekibi yok sayma.

### “Tell me about a time you interpreted unclear regulation.”

**UK / Brexit / UKCA** — en güçlü hikâyen.

> After Brexit, the UK extended the use of CE marking, and at the same time there was a product-safety standard transition. Internally people said: if we can still CE-mark, maybe we don’t need the UK construction change. That change had a cost, so the commercial pressure was real.
>
> My reading was: the marking route does not freeze the safety requirements of the market. But I couldn’t push that on opinion. I went through OPSS guidance, I spoke with certification bodies — they didn’t all agree — and then I put our exact product scenario to AMDEA and OPSS. Once we had a consistent official reading, I aligned Vestel UK and the technical teams, and I ran the transition for about 300,000 products.
>
> I pulled the technical switch date one month earlier because of supply risk, I planned stock burn-down, and I added a large UKCA packing label so old and new builds couldn’t mix under the same product code. We finished with no production or shipment stop. That project is why I’m confident in grey regulatory areas.

---

### “A time you stopped something unpopular.”

**Çin geçişi — yanlış numune / etiketsiz üretim.**

> During a China safety and energy transition we had about 100 models and 30 customers. Some customers wanted to send existing stock straight to the lab. The units were not built to the new standard. I refused, because a failed test is not only a fail — it’s lost samples, lost weeks, lost money.
>
> I also held Chinese production until the new energy registration and label were actually in place. That’s uncomfortable when sales has volume. The result was we didn’t put the wrong grade or the wrong construction on the market, and we used the same window to add alternative critical components so we weren’t single-sourced.

### “A time you saved the company from a serious commercial loss.”

**BAE G-Mark / ihale.**

> A customer had a UAE tender. The request came late, production was already done, and the G-Mark had expired. If the goods hit customs without a valid mark, they bounce. If they miss the tender date, the customer pays penalties and we take the damage.
>
> I sat with the customer and completed the paperwork in the meeting. I pulled R&D and quality into a working session and we produced in two hours what normally takes a week. I got the certification body on a call the same day, then I went to GSO with the urgency and the file. We finished in about one-fifth of the normal time, before shipment. No customs rejection, no penalty.

---

### “A time you improved a process.”

**Aylık sertifika takibi + 5.000 kod etiket.**

> There was no system that told us which certificates would expire next month. I built a monthly list of live certificates, expiry, and upcoming standard transitions, and I circulated it. At year end I reported new certificates, updates, and how many units those certificates actually covered. Complaints from sales on Gulf work dropped because people could see the pipeline.
>
> Separately I found rated-current inconsistencies across about 5,000 live codes. I cleaned the data with sales and customers. Unexciting work. It’s also how you survive an audit.

---

### “A time you led people who knew the product better than you.”

**Makine mühendisleri, soğutma tasarımı.**

> In 2025 I was asked to lead the non-EU cooling design team together with certification — six people, mostly mechanical engineers. I am not a mechanical design engineer. That created resistance, and I understand why.
>
> I didn’t fake coil design. I used what I did know: energy standards, country rules, certification constraints, and project discipline. I stayed calm, I set a clear plan, we delivered two US designs, about 50 market adaptations and 50 version consolidations. After a year the work was in a much more stable rhythm. I would do the same at NIBE: I won’t pretend to be your heat-pump designer. I will make sure design decisions are certifiable.

### “Biggest mistake / what would you do differently?”

Körfez geçişi — malzeme tedariki ve FIFO. Olgun cevap.

> The Gulf 2022 safety transition was seen as a success. I still think about what could have gone wrong. Material supply slipped and we had to move the date. There was no FIFO, and we kept the same product code for old and new builds. We managed it by pulling 30,000 old units through, but the volume was still manageable.
>
> On the UK project I didn’t wait for that lesson twice. I brought the technical date forward, I planned stock, and I added a visual mark on the pack. My mistake wasn’t the Gulf plan on paper — it was not designing hard enough for supply and warehouse reality. I don’t want a clean procedure that fails in the yard.
